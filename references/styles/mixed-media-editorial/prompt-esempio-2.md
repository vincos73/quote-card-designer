Design Style:
  Name: "Mixed Media Editorial"
  Concept: >
    Expressive editorial photomontage combines photographic cutouts, flat
    graphic shapes and deliberately assembled typography. Images retain
    their separate identities: different scales, cut edges, paper borders
    and overlaps create a playful visual argument. The work feels authored,
    graphic and physically assembled, even when produced digitally. Its
    identity comes from these relationships rather than a fixed colour palette.

  Mood:
    - Expressive
    - Playful
    - Editorial
    - Graphic
    - Handmade
    - Unexpected
    - Slightly irreverent

  Color Palette:
    Fixed Colors: false
    Strategy: >
      Select colours for each card from its content and the user's preferences.
      Colour does not define this style. A quiet neutral field, a saturated
      field or a predominantly monochrome treatment can all support the same
      collage language. No particular hue or fluorescent palette is mandatory.
    Roles:
      Ground: >
        One dominant flat field or a small set of broad paper-like planes
        establishes the composition and separates the main masses.
      Graphic Accent: >
        A contrasting colour or tonal value gives selected bursts, circles,
        strips or counterweights their role in the composition.
      Cutout Separation: >
        White, paper tone or a palette colour may outline a cutout. Choose it
        for separation from the ground rather than as a fixed neon treatment.
      Text and Paper: >
        Maintain clear contrast between printed letters, paper slips and the
        field. Dark-on-light and light-on-dark treatments are both available.
      Photographic Layer: >
        Predominantly black-and-white or desaturated photographic reproductions
        form the reference language. Their tonal contrast matters more than
        the surrounding hue.
    Selection: >
      Keep the palette coherent and limited enough that photographs, text and
      graphic accents remain distinguishable. Choose colours afresh; do not
      inherit the colours of one reference panel as a permanent preset.

      Role: "A quotation integrated alongside the photographic protagonist"
    Display:
      Style: "Large assertive sans-serif or condensed poster lettering"
      Character: >

    Hierarchy: >
      Choose a primary typographic treatment for the card; do not combine all
      registers by default. Let the image carry a substantial visual role,
      while keeping the full quotation readable on a phone. Preserve the
      original case, accents and punctuation. Paper-slip composition does not
      require random ransom-note lettering.

  Layout:
    Structure:
      - "Asymmetrical editorial composition with one clear focal relationship"
      - "Visible layering of photographs, printed type and flat graphic planes"
      - "Expressive scale differences and selective overlap"
      - "A balance between concentrated activity and a quieter open field"
    Relationships: >
      Make the photographic cutouts, graphic accents and words interact.
      A burst can sit behind a silhouette; an oversized object can anchor a
      smaller figure; an image module can repeat at different angles; a paper
      phrase can follow an open interval beside a protagonist. Choose the
      relationship from the current content rather than scattering decorations.
    Composition Families:
      Narrative Montage: >
        Combine a small set of figures or objects at contrasting scales into
        an intentional, sometimes impossible interaction.
      Iconic Cutout: >
        Let one dominant photographic figure or object lead, supported by
        graphic counterweights and a readable typographic assembly.
      Paired Stickers: >
        Use two cutouts with a shared outline treatment and contrasting poses,
        orientations or scales, leaving clear intervals for the words.
      Repeated Module: >
        Repeat a photographic or object-shaped module with controlled changes
        of angle, scale or overlap. Repetition belongs to the imagery unless
        repetition of the supplied words is explicitly requested.
    Selection: >
      These are alternative compositional tools, not four required sections.
      Select a coherent family or a purposeful combination appropriate to the
      text. Avoid turning every card into the same central quote-and-icon layout.

      Place an accent behind, beside or partly under a cutout to create

  Effects:

    Imperfection: "Controlled cut edges, small rotations and deliberate print variation"
